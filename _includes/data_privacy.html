The details, for families, schools and data protection officers

  • Who is responsible: the University of Leeds is the data controller.
  • Legal basis: public task (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e)); for special category data, such as health records and information about wellbeing, research in the public interest (Article 9(2)(j)). The study’s data protection impact assessment was signed off by the University in May 2026.
  • How long we keep it: research data for up to 10 years after the project ends, in line with University of Leeds research data guidance. Names and contact details are kept apart from research data.
  • Where it is kept: information given on this website, including screenshots, is stored for the University in Google Cloud’s London region. Linked records stay within Connected West Yorkshire’s secure systems.
  • How we improve this website: we count, anonymously, which pages and parts of pages are read and for how long, which links are followed, and where people stop or need to fix something in the online forms, so that we can make them easier to use. Apart from remembering if you choose not to be counted, nothing is stored on your device, and it doesn’t record who you are, your IP address or anything you type. The counts are kept for five years, then deleted.
  • How linking works: for young people who take part in the workshop, the study’s information (the EEG recording, the attention task results, the survey answers and, from the online form, the parent’s answers and any screenshots) can be linked with GP records, hospital records, community and mental health services records (including CAMHS), and Department for Education records of attendance, attainment and special educational needs. The University of Leeds team holds names, dates of birth and addresses separately; an independent organisation carries out the matching; researchers see only a study code. More in the information sheet.
  • Your rights: to be told how information is used; to see the information we hold and get a copy; to have anything wrong corrected; to ask for it to be deleted; to ask us to limit how it is used; and to object to it being used. Some of these rights can be limited when information is used for research in the public interest, for example once it has been made anonymous; if so, we will explain why.
  • Complaints: please tell us first. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO), the UK’s independent regulator for data protection: ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or 0303 123 1113.
  • Questions about your data or your rights: the University’s data protection team, dpo@leeds.ac.uk. About the study: {{ site.email }}.
