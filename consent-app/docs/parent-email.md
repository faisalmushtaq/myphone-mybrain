# Email to parents and carers, for schools to send

For the school to send to parents and carers of the classes taking part,
two to three weeks before the workshop. Fill in the parts in [square
brackets]. Each school has its own link: Dixons Unity Academy
`myphonemybrain.com/dua`, The Grammar School at Leeds
`myphonemybrain.com/gsal`. The link takes parents straight to the form with
the school already filled in.

Updated 19 October 2026 with the two kinds of opt-out (`opt-outs.md`):
the data-only option is not yet in the approved information sheet, so it
needs the ethics amendment before this version goes out.

Written 17 October 2026 in plain words to match the information sheet
(`/information/`) and the form. Check with the ethics approval (SoPREC 4202)
before sending: recruitment emails may need to be the approved version.

---

**Subject:** MyPhone/MyBrain: a 10-minute form about your child’s phone, and the brain science workshop

Dear parent or carer,

On [date], researchers from the University of Leeds are coming to [school
name] to run a brain science workshop with [year group(s)] during the
school day. It is part of MyPhone/MyBrain, a study of how phones and
digital life relate to young people’s brain development, learning and
wellbeing.

**One thing we ask of you: a 10-minute form**

Please fill in the short online form as soon as you can:

**[myphonemybrain.com/dua or myphonemybrain.com/gsal]**

It asks a few questions about your child’s phone. If you are happy to,
you can also send screenshots of the phone’s screen-time page. Those show
which apps were used and for how long. They don’t show messages, photos
or what was watched, and you can cover up any part before sending.

Most people guess their screen time wrongly, often by hours. The phone’s
own record is the best way to see how phones really affect young people,
so your screenshots help a great deal.

Sharing is your choice. Your child’s name is never kept with the
screenshots, and the school never sees them. Not sure? The form is saved
as you go, and you can finish it later with the reference it gives you.

**The workshop**

Your child is already invited, and you don’t need to do anything for
them to take part. In the workshop, young people learn how the brain
works and how scientists study it. They can also take part in a short,
safe brain recording (EEG), a simple computer task and some
questionnaires. On the day, the researchers explain everything again and
ask each young person if they want to take part. Your child can say no,
or stop at any time, without giving a reason.

If you would rather your child didn’t take part, you have two choices:

* **They join the workshop, but their information isn’t used.** Your
  child takes part with their class as normal, but nothing recorded about
  them is kept or used for the study, and nothing is linked with their
  school or health records.
* **They don’t take part, and their information isn’t used.** Your child
  does something else at school while their class does the workshop.

For either one, email **brainpop@leeds.ac.uk** before [date], saying which
you would like, with your child’s name, school and class or year group.
There is no form or slip to return, and we reply
to confirm. Please also email us, or tell the school, if your child
has ever had epilepsy or seizures. They can still join the workshop, but
won’t do the brain recording.

**More information**

Everything about the study, including how information is kept safe, is at
**myphonemybrain.com/information**. Questions are welcome at
brainpop@leeds.ac.uk.

Thank you for your help.

[Name]
[School name]

On behalf of the MyPhone/MyBrain team, School of Psychology, University of
Leeds
